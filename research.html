History of Jyotisha

Jyotisha developed alongside Indian calendrical and astronomical traditions. Its early practical work focused on tracking time, seasons, lunar cycles and ritual calendars. Later texts developed systems for modelling celestial positions and interpreting them through cultural frameworks.

Nakshatras

The zodiac is divided into 27 lunar mansions, each covering 13°20′ of the ecliptic. The divisions give a finer-grained way to describe the Moon's apparent path and are central to many traditional calendars and interpretive systems.

Rashi

Rashi are twelve 30° divisions of the 360° zodiac. Each division has a Sanskrit name and a commonly used English correspondence. In the generator, the birth date is mapped to a simple educational sign profile.

Panchanga

Panchanga means five limbs: tithi, vara, nakshatra, yoga and karana. Together they form a traditional calendrical lens for describing a day, combining solar and lunar cycles with weekday and angular relationships.

Indian Astronomy

Indian astronomical works used mathematical tables, geometric reasoning and observation to calculate planetary and lunar positions. These traditions contributed to timekeeping and calendar-making across many regions and historical periods.
